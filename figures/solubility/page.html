Solubility — how things dissolve

A solution is a mix where one thing spreads out evenly in another. The solute dissolves. The solvent does the dissolving. Solute + solvent = solution.

How to read a curve

1. Find the temperature on the bottom line.

2. Go up to the curve. Read the grams on the left. That is the most that can dissolve in 100 g of water.

3. A steep curve (KNO₃) means heat changes a lot. A flat curve (NaCl) means heat changes very little.

4. A curve that goes down (Ce₂(SO₄)₃) means less dissolves when hot. Gases also go down.

Where curves cross (KNO₃ and NaCl near 22 °C), both dissolve the same amount.

Data: g of solute per 100 g of solvent at 1 atm, from standard school tables (CRC Handbook). Iodine and Ce₂(SO₄)₃ values are approximate. Built with the Anatomy kit.
