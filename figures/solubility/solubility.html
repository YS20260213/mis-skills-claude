Solubility Lab

A solution is a mix where one thing spreads out evenly inside another. The solute is the thing that dissolves. The solvent is the thing it dissolves in. Solute + solvent = solution. Pick a solvent and a solute, change the heat, and watch the particles.

The solubility curve

Solubility is how much solute can dissolve in 100 g of solvent at one temperature. The line shows the limit. Below the line = unsaturated (more can dissolve). On the line = saturated (full). Above the line = supersaturated (too much, not stable) or solid left at the bottom.

Types of solutions

A solution can be a gas, a liquid or a solid. The solvent is the part you have the most of, and it gives the solution its state.

How full is a solution?

Unsaturated

Room for more

It has less solute than it can hold. If you add more, it dissolves.
Saturated

Full

It holds the most it can at this temperature. Extra solute stays at the bottom. Particles still leave and join the crystal at the same speed (a dynamic equilibrium).
Supersaturated

Too full

It holds more than the limit. You make it by heating, dissolving, then cooling very slowly. One small crystal makes the extra solute come out fast.
Dilute vs concentrated

A little or a lot

Dilute = a little solute in a lot of solvent. Concentrated = a lot of solute. These words compare; they are not exact.

Temperature and pressure

Solids in water

Hot = usually more

Most solids (sugar, KNO₃) dissolve more when the water is hot. Hot particles move fast and hit the crystal harder. Salt changes only a little. A few solids, like Ce₂(SO₄)₃, dissolve less when hot.
Gases in water

Hot = less

Gases like CO₂ escape when the water is hot. That is why warm soda goes flat faster than cold soda.
Pressure

Only for gases

More pressure pushes more gas into the liquid. A closed soda bottle has high pressure. Open it, the pressure drops, and bubbles come out.

Like dissolves like

Water is polar: the oxygen side is a little negative (δ−) and the hydrogen side is a little positive (δ+). Polar solvents pull on polar or charged particles. Nonpolar solvents (hexane, oil, gasoline) have no charged sides, so they mix with other nonpolar things.

What makes it dissolve faster?

🥄 Stirring

Brings fresh solvent to the crystal.

🔥 Heat

Particles move faster and hit harder.

🧂 Small pieces

Powder has more surface than a big lump.

⚠️ Remember

Stirring and small pieces change the speed, not the amount. Only temperature (and pressure for gases) changes how much can dissolve.
